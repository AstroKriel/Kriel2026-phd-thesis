    {\large\bfseries Examiner 3}

Examiner 3 provided a detailed report, with two connected observations and a large number of smaller wording, notation, and referencing suggestions. I have grouped my response into five categories.

\textit{\textbf{Consistency across paper-chapters}}

The majority of their comments highlighted inconsistent notation and cross-references across my paper-chapters, which were largely a by-product of the papers being published in different journals (each with their own conventions). I have now standardised the notation in both the first two paper-chapters, and added a table of symbols (to complement the note on notation) in the third paper-chapter, since its subject, and thus notation, is different.

\textit{\textbf{References in the introduction}}

I have also responded to their general criticism that some references were missing in the introduction. Since they had not explicitly indicated which parts of the introduction were lacking citations, or which citations they believed were missing, I have cited a few recent papers that I believed were relevant, and in the few cases where relevant reviews were not already cited, I have now cited them.

\textit{\textbf{Kinetic effects and anisotropic viscosity}}

They also highlighted two places in the thesis, \S1.3.4 (Figure 1.7) and \S2.4.6.2, where the introduction to the thesis could be extended to connect with the surrounding theory of kinetic effects and anisotropic viscosity. I have read the literature that they had recommended, as well as some other relevant work, and have now incorporated a summary of this in \S1.3.1.

\textit{\textbf{Comments on already-published chapters}}

A small number of comments requested further explanation, derivation, or clarification of material in Chapters 2 and 3, which reproduce two papers already published in peer-reviewed journals (Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society and Physical Review E, respectively). Moreover, Examiner 3 noted that comments of this kind were about the coherence of the thesis, rather than a requirement: ``a paper collection might in principle be acceptable for the award of a PhD.'' I have therefore left the published text unchanged in these instances, so that they continue to reflect the accepted version. Their feedback is nonetheless valuable, and will carry forward with me in my future work.

\textit{\textbf{Chapter 4 updated to its current manuscript}}

A number of comments concerned Chapter 4, which is adapted from a manuscript in preparation. Since the underlying manuscript has been revised since the version this thesis was originally based on, I have updated Chapter 4 to its current state, which directly resolves these comments.
